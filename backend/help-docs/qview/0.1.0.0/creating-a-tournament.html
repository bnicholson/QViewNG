The most fundamental data in QView is the Tournament. Nearly everything in QView has to do with some Tournament in some way, shape or form.

To create Tournaments your user must first be approved for Tournament creation capabilities. See more on becoming approved to be a Tournament Manager here.

Creating the Tournament

To start, Tournament creation can be initiated from two locations:

  1. From your user profile, under Managed Tournaments, click Create Tournament:
  2. From the Home page, click Create a New Tournament (if you don’t see this option, you’re either not logged in or you don’t have Tournament Manager capabilities):

Both locations will open the Create / Edit Tournament form. Fill out the form, then click Save.

Most of the fields are self-explanatory. The ones worth a second look:

  • Name must be unique across QView. QuizMachine also matches the Tournament by name, so avoid renaming it once Rooms are connected.
  • Start Date and End Date are required, and the start can’t be after the end.
  • Default Round Duration (minutes) (30 by default) is how long each Round is taken to last. It’s used when you create a schedule or edit Round start times in bulk, and to show the Breaks between Rounds on the Schedule page (see Post-Registration Prep: Rounds and Games).
  • Registration Types turns the Team, Gear and Volunteer registration tabs on or off. Untick all three to turn registration off completely. Registration Open Date and Registration Close Date set the Registration Window (see Setting Up Divisions and Registration).
  • Short Information is the one line people see when searching for Tournaments, and in QuizMachine when a Room picks its Tournament.

The Visibility dropdown is used to control whether the Tournament can be seen publicly - by visitors and everyone else. New Tournaments start as Private. If it is set to “Private” only you (and the Tournament’s admins) can view the Tournament, from your profile’s “Managed Tournaments” section. For private Tournaments that you manage, you can click this button on the Home page to navigate there when logged in as a Tournament Manager:

QuizMachine can only see Public Tournaments. Make the Tournament public before tournament day, or the Rooms won’t be able to find it.

At the bottom of the form is what is called a Markdown editor. It is initialized with template text in the Markdown format. You can edit its contents on the left and view the changes on the right side of the editor.

For help with writing Markdown, QView includes a Markdown syntax cheatsheet. To access it, just click the word “Markdown” and the cheatsheet will open in a new tab.

When you click Save, you’ll be asked to confirm the information. Click Confirm to create the Tournament.

You will then be directed to the Tournament’s Overview page, which looks something like this:

The title is shown on its own line with an Edit button, which only you and the Tournament Admins you add can see. Use it whenever you need to change any of the details above.

It then shows a few data fields that QView tracks uniquely and for different purposes. Finally, the Markdown portion which shares all other relevant information is included in this Overview page.

What is the Pairing Code?

QView gives every new Tournament a 6-digit Pairing Code. Each Room’s QuizMachine needs it to connect to the Tournament (see Setting Up Rooms using QuizMachine + QView). It’s hidden as ######## on the Overview page; click the eye icon to show it. Only Tournament Managers and admins can see it.

You can change it in the Edit form. Changing it doesn’t disconnect Rooms that are already paired, but every Room that pairs afterwards needs the new code. Only change it if the code has leaked.

Adding Buildings (optional)

If your Tournament is spread across more than one building, Buildings let you group your Rooms so people can find them. Once your Rooms are assigned to Buildings, anyone viewing the Tournament’s Schedule can filter it by Building: choose Room under Filter By, then pick a Building to narrow the Room list to just that Building’s Rooms. You can skip this for a single-building Tournament.

  1. In the Tournament sidebar, click Buildings.
  2. Click Create Building.
  3. Enter the Building Name and any Notes (for example, where to park or which entrance to use).
  4. Click Save and confirm.

Click a Building’s name to open its page. Its Rooms tab lists the Rooms in that Building, and Rooms you create from there are put in that Building automatically.

Adding Rooms for the Tournament

Rooms don’t have to be added to your Tournament until just before creating Games (commonly known as 20-question quiz rounds), however, Rooms are associated directly with Tournaments and can be used across all Divisions of the Tournament.

For this reason, it’s worth creating your Rooms now, to see how large your Tournament can scale to with addition of more and more Teams when they register.

First, in your new Tournament, in the sidebar click Rooms to navigate to the Rooms list.

Then click Create Room:

Provide the name of the Room and perhaps some comments about it: whether it’s in a certain building, in a certain hard-to-find location in a building, or what its identifiable characteristics are (like “room with accordion divider”, “sanctuary” or “first room on the right when entering building’s main entrance”). The form also has:

  • Building: the Building the Room is in, if you added Buildings. Leave it as None otherwise. Assigning a Building is optional, but it lets people filter the Tournament’s Schedule by Building (see Adding Buildings above).
  • Supports 3-team games: tick this if the Room has the seats and Gear for three Teams.

You can use any Room-naming scheme that you prefer. Many choose to label Rooms with letters (‘A’, ‘B’, ‘C’, etc.). You can use numbers, although you may prefer to keep numbering for Rounds only, to keep a clear separation of what is what (letters = Rooms, numbers = Rounds). If Rooms have their own names already (such as can be found on a college campus), then you may wish to use that (i.e. “Benson 203” could be “BEN203” - or you could just use “Benson 203”) - or you could name the Rooms something fun! Just make sure everyone knows where to go to get to where they need to be when they need to be there.

QuizMachine matches each Room by its name, so settle on Room names before tournament day. If you rename a Room after its QuizMachine has connected, reconnect that Room.

You can always come back and edit these later, including assigning a Room you’ve already created to a Building. Once a Room exists, its Edit form also lets you assign its Quizmaster and Content Judge.
